CASE NO. 2021-EWHC-2987 (Comm)
RAPIDSHIP LOGISTICS LTD v. PEAKCOMMERCE PLC
High Court of Justice, Queen's Bench Division, Commercial Court
Filed: December 3, 2021

JUDGMENT

FACTS

PeakCommerce PLC ("PeakCommerce"), an e-commerce platform operator, entered into a logistics services agreement (the "Agreement") with RapidShip Logistics Ltd ("RapidShip") on June 1, 2021, for the delivery of packages during the peak holiday season from October through December 2021.

The Agreement specified that RapidShip would deliver 10,000 packages within agreed timeframes. Section 9.2 of the Agreement contained the following liquidated damages provision:

"In the event of delay in delivery, the Service Provider shall pay to the Customer liquidated damages in the amount of £50 per package per day of delay, as a genuine pre-estimate of the loss that would be suffered by the Customer."

RapidShip delivered 2,000 packages on time but was 5 days late on the remaining 8,000 packages due to vehicle fleet breakdowns and driver shortages. PeakCommerce asserted a liquidated damages claim of £2,000,000 (8,000 packages × £50 × 5 days).

RapidShip argues that the clause is an unenforceable penalty.

ANALYSIS

I. The Legal Framework: Liquidated Damages vs. Penalties

The law regarding liquidated damages and penalties was significantly clarified by the Supreme Court in Cavendish Square Holding BV v Makdessi [2015] UKSC 67 and ParkingEye Ltd v Beavis [2015] UKSC 67. The Supreme Court departed from the traditional Dunlop test and held that a clause will be unenforceable as a penalty if it imposes a detriment on the contract-breaker out of all proportion to any legitimate interest of the innocent party in the enforcement of the primary obligation.

The test is whether the clause is "extravagant, exorbitant, or unconscionable" having regard to the innocent party's legitimate interest.

II. PeakCommerce's Legitimate Interest

PeakCommerce has a legitimate interest beyond mere compensation for direct financial loss. Late deliveries during the peak holiday season cause:

1. Direct financial loss: refund requests, chargebacks, and lost repeat customers
2. Reputational damage: negative reviews during the most visible sales period
3. Contractual liability: PeakCommerce's own obligations to sellers on its platform
4. Customer acquisition cost: the cost of acquiring each customer averaged £45, and late deliveries result in permanent customer loss

PeakCommerce presented evidence that its average revenue per customer per holiday season was £180, and that 60% of customers who experienced late delivery did not return. The estimated actual loss from 8,000 late deliveries was approximately £864,000 in lost customer lifetime value, plus £120,000 in direct refunds and chargebacks — totaling approximately £984,000.

III. Proportionality Analysis

The liquidated damages claim of £2,000,000 is approximately twice the estimated actual loss of £984,000. The court must assess whether this disparity renders the clause a penalty.

The £50 per package per day rate, when applied to the specific facts of this case, produces a result that is disproportionate to PeakCommerce's legitimate interest. While a daily rate mechanism is commercially reasonable for incentivizing timely performance, the cumulative effect of £50 × 5 days = £250 per package significantly exceeds the average customer value of £180.

However, the court notes that the clause was freely negotiated between sophisticated commercial parties of comparable bargaining power. Neither party was a consumer. RapidShip had the opportunity to negotiate the rate and accepted it.

IV. Comparison: English vs. American Approach

For reference, American courts apply the Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 356, which requires that liquidated damages must be reasonable in light of the anticipated or actual loss and that the harm must be difficult to prove. Under the UCC § 2-718, the amount must be reasonable in light of anticipated or actual harm. Several US courts have struck down per-day liquidated damages clauses where the cumulative amount bore no reasonable relationship to foreseeable damages.

V. Determination

Applying the Cavendish Square framework, the court finds that the liquidated damages clause, while producing a high result in this case, is not "extravagant, exorbitant, or unconscionable" when considered against PeakCommerce's broad legitimate interests, including reputational harm and lost customer lifetime value.

However, the court exercises its discretion to reduce the damages to reflect the five-day delay more proportionately. The court awards £1,200,000 — representing a rate of £30 per package per day for 8,000 packages over 5 days, which the court considers proportionate to PeakCommerce's legitimate interests.

ORDER

1. The liquidated damages clause in Section 9.2 is ENFORCEABLE in principle.
2. Damages are assessed at £1,200,000, reflecting a proportionate adjustment.
3. RapidShip shall pay £1,200,000 to PeakCommerce within 28 days.

SO ORDERED.
